Talking to My Eight Year Old Self
                                                               by Navya Gupta

You ran untamed-
not in chaos, but like freedom yet to be touched by shame.
 Legs streaked with mud, palms scraped raw with gravel, 
your eyes lit up like mischief was a language
and you spoke it without stuttering.

You wore your father's eyes not just expressive,
but sky wide and storm honest.
And your mother's laughter
soft, alive, like breeze on that autumn day 
You carried both not yet knowing the weight of that inheritance.

Every two years
the universe hit reset. New streets.New people.
New skin you had to grow into. 
You hated that part.
The boxes. The goodbyes. 
The blank slate beginnings.
But a part of you
would soon start craving it
the rush of becoming someone new. 
The quiet steel it built inside you, while no one clapped.

You will learn to walk into cafeterias like battlefields.
To lead with kindness
even when your name didn't echo back

To turn strangers into people of utmost importance 
and let them go when the page turned.
Because no one could stay, not for long.
But you will hold them anyways, won't you? 
In pixelated photos that time didn't erase.
In that quiet sting whispering
"You saw me when I was still forming"

You are quiet.
But not weak.
The kind of quiet that doesn't ask for applause
But one that the crowds will keep standing for anyway. You tried to be everything.
Good.
Soft.
Sharp.
Unshakeable.
You will want to be easy to hold without folding yourself to fit. 
God, that will hurt.

Then the books will come.
Not like guests, but like storms they will take you whole.
One day you'll be just a girl in warm light, the next?
A girl scattered across galaxies. Reading would never be running it would be rising.
It will stretch your mind open Give your silence shapes
 
Let you live a hundred different lives without moving an inch
Those novels?
They will save you
before you even know what from.

And by then?
You'll be an adult whatever that means

You want to know the truth? Being an adult is loud and lonely. It's quiet and full.
It's contradiction after contradiction wrapped in deadlines and unspoken grief.


It's smiling in group photos
with hands that haven't stopped shaking 
since morning It's walking into a room
and forgetting why you came in metaphorically, 
and too often, literally.

It's taking care of people
even when you can't take care of yourself.
It's nights that don't end even when the sun comes up.
It's missing versions of yourself
you didn't realise you were allowed to mourn.
It's loving and losing
and still waking up with the capacity to hope 
That's the wildest part.

Because adulthood will your heart
in ways that don't look like heartbreak. 
Silent ways
Lonely ways
Like when you achieve something and no one claps.
Like when you finally rest and feel guilty for it.

But it's not just pain
It's watching the sky turn pink and pausing.
It's laughing over cheap coffee with people who get it. 
It's long walks that heal more than therapy.
It's doing something scared
and then realizing no one else ever had a map either. 
It's failing beautifully.

It's choosing softness
even after life teaches you sharpness.
It's learning that strength doesn't mean 
not crying it means crying and going to work anyway.
It's knowing that you will lose yourself. Again and again.
But each time, you'll come back. Brighter.
Wiser.
A little more yours.

So here's what I'd tell you, 
if you were eight and sitting in front of me,
 knees scabbed, eyes full of questions.
Don't stop being wild.

Don't shrink to fit their comfort.
Don't stop singing, even when the world feels too loud. Don't rush.
There is no finish line.
And above all
You will never be too much. You never were.
You were just becoming.

And my God, look at you now.

He pocketed his phone that previously 
was balanced in the neck and shoulder zone.

He flashed a note, 500 rupees, 
but the reluctant cop did not accept with ease.
"At least rupees thousand," he said.

And Ramu had to accept or he was dead. 
Raju saw his friend pay.
Don't do it, he wanted to say. 
He knew Ramu was wrong. It wasn't right.
Ramu could hurt someone in the dark night.

No difference, situation would be the same.

The best one he has to seek.

that could cut short his long office day?
A contractor offered Rs. 10,000, that's all. 
Mr. Verma held his breath on the call.
The money would be transferred directly to 
his Swiss bank account.
The government would never know of the amount, 
but Mr. Verma would never accept bribe --
only Rs. 1 Lakh? -- he could not let. After bargaining, Rs. 5 Lakh was all set, might help him pay off his personal debt.

His actions, however, were not a new thing. Everyday, 
Officers new bribes would bring.

He too, was completely laid.
The new road project was to be made.
He remembered how his UPXC result filled his family with pride,
and became an IAX officer,
watching his colleagues make money on the side. 
Anti-corruption he tried to defend,
but doing so, he did repent.
His career might even come to an end,
or once again somewhere else, his transfer would send. 
A hundred corrupt officers he saw every day,
but nothing he could do or say.

The MP of the constituency would make him pay. 
This time, he earned himself a transfer in May.
They threatened not to meddle, and not to fight, 
to protect any citizen's money or rights.
The transfer was a warning, he knew it. 
They might even kill him, if he blew it.

After making sure he was alone,

Sweat was seen on Panditji's forehead

 Only death, perhaps weeping families

After all, he was paid 12 crore.

The accused offered two crore, that's all.

so much so that the judge had been reduced to tears.
A land case, the most common between a family of angry Brahmans. 
"All right," XYZ replied,
bribe -- the only source of income on which he relied. 
Justice ABC saw everything,
but he couldn't say anything.
What good would come out if he accused? That might even destroy him, he mused. How is it that the very upholders of law, Go against it -- the thought started to gnaw. What use is it to go to court,

When for money, humanity we abort? What use is it to witness a vice,
when even with a hammer in hand, we can't raise our voice? What point in being a judge, when bribe is so usual we don't even budge? Why are we addressed as justice in our names, when to follow it we are afraid to blame? Indians they say, and yet what pride,

 Why Indians, what a shame,

AFTER THE RAIN
                                         by Vinita Rk

Life is not a story,
it’s a battlefield—
and some of us walk through it
barefoot,
bleeding,
pretending the cuts are just grime, just dirt.

I have loved like fire—
wild, blinding,
the kind that warms bones
and burns homes
in the same breath.

And I have lost with the heaviness of rain
that never learns when to stop falling.

There are days I’ve screamed into pillows,
and days I’ve smiled through splinters of myself—
because life demands performances
even when the soul is shaking.

People clap for survival
but never sit through the rehearsals
where you crumble,
again and again,
in the dark.

I have been held,
and I have been abandoned—
both felt like being struck by lightning,
just from different directions.

Love taught me softness.
Loss taught me the truth.
And somewhere between those two storms,
I learned how to stand alone,
without collapsing.

Life is brutal like that—
it gives you hands to touch the sky
and knees that buckle
on the same day.

It gifts you people
as if they’re constellations,
then watches them fade
until you can’t remember
which star was yours.

But I am still here.

Not untouched,
never untouched,
but undefeated,
In the quiet, stubborn way
only the broken can understand.

I’ve buried versions of myself,
I once thought were forever.

I’ve mourned futures,
I never got to live.

And still, somehow,
the heart keeps beating—
clumsy, scarred,
but impossibly loyal
to the idea of tomorrow.

Life has claws.
It drags, scrapes, tears—
but it also rebuilds.

Every ruin I’ve stood inside
has let wildflowers rise-
Stubborn and alive.

I’ve learned
that love is worth the risk,
that loss is proof we felt deeply,
that grief is just love
looking for a place to go.

And I would choose
to feel it all again—
every ache, every bruise, every burn—
because numbness is a prison
and I want to be free, I want to live.

So here I stand,
not as the hero,
not as the victim,
but as the one who stayed.

Through the fire, the night,
the leaving, the returning,
the chaos, the quiet,
and every impossible thing
in between.

If life is a storm,
then let it come.

I have been the ocean before,
I know how to rise.

EVERYTHING IS A SIN                                         by Zaden



They called Lilith a demon ‘cause she refused to bow,
Head held high, no halo, no vow.
Not God’s puppet, not Adam’s toy,
She craved her power, not borrowed joy.
She wanted pleasure, they called her whore,
She wanted a throne, they shut the door.
Tell me, if lust is sin, why’s Aphrodite pure?
If wisdom’s sacred, why must Athena endure?
They call it divinity when it fits their crown,
But when a woman dares , they burn her down.

They called Medusa a monster, just ‘cause she bled,
They never tell you what Poseidon said.
Her scream became stone, her silence a blade,
Athena turned justice into shame’s parade.
She wanted peace, they gave her a curse,
Called her wicked for being hurt.
If survival’s sin, then what’s divine?
If her pain’s a myth, then what’s your shrine?
They say she kills men with just a stare 
Maybe it’s guilt they see reflected there.

They called Hecate a witch for knowing too much,
For holding torches gods couldn’t touch.
She spoke to ghosts, they called it wrong,
But silence is death and she was strong.
Three faces to see what mortals deny,
The moon her crown, the truth her sky.
They feared her power, her words, her art,
They burned her name but not her heart.
And still her flame lights every crossroad,
Every woman alone in the dark she showed.

They called Persephone captive, queen of decay,
But no one asked if she wanted to stay.
Half in sunlight, half in hell,
She wore both worlds and wore them well.
They said she was stolen but maybe she chose,
The darkness, the silence, the bloom, the rose.
She learned that heaven was just a cell,
And hell, at least, was honest as hell.
For what’s a goddess without her scars?
What’s spring without the darkened stars?

They called Pandora cursed for being curious,
Her box of sins, too luxurious.
But tell me~ if curiosity is crime,
Why did God make Eve with a questioning mind?
Why give thought if it’s meant to be caged?
Why teach love if truth is enraged?
They gave her hope, but called her vain,
Made her the mother of mortal pain.
And yet it’s her defiance that makes us whole,
Every question a rebellion of the soul.

They called Lucifer fallen for wanting light,
For loving God with too much might.
He craved the truth, not blind adore,
So Heaven closed its golden door.
They said he burned, but he just shone,
They feared a star brighter than the throne.
If rebellion’s sin, then faith is rot,
For only questions prove there’s thought.
So he fell, not damned but free
And hell became what heaven couldn’t be.

They called Prometheus cursed for giving fire,
For lifting men from God’s empire.
Chained to stone, with bloodied breath,
He fed mankind and feasted on death.
If mercy’s punished, what’s divine?
If gods are cruel, why worship the shrine?
He burned for us, not out of greed,
A savior born from a rebel’s deed.
And still his flame in every mind,
Whispers be defiant, be kind.

They called Eve sinner for tasting the fruit,
But wasn’t knowledge the truest root?
She bit the truth and bore the blame,
And birthed the world through holy shame.
Adam obeyed, she dared to see,
And they cursed her name for eternity.
But maybe Eden was the lie,
And her sin was learning why.

They called every rebel wicked and wild,
Every godless thought a feral child.
They carved halos from control and fear,
And wrote damnation on the ones who hear.
But what if sin was never wrong?
What if the devil just sang too strong?
What if heaven’s rules were human made,
To keep the bold and brave afraid?

They said murder’s sin, ‘cause they decreed,
But what if death was sacred creed?
If worship meant to spill, not pray,
Would killers be saints today?
Morality’s a crown they bend,
A means to rule, not to mend.
There is no holy, no profane 
Just different names for the same pain.

So call her demon, call him beast,
Call them cursed, call them deceased.
But every god you cast aside,
Was just the truth you learned to hide.
Lilith, Medusa, Hecate they burned,
Yet through their ashes the world has turned.
Each curse a hymn, each scar divine,
Each fall a sermon redefined.

So don’t preach heaven to me again,
Your saints are just sinners in polished chains.
You build your temples on bones and blame,
Then call rebellion a godless flame.
But maybe hell is not below,
It’s in the hands that claim to know.
And maybe sin’s the only way
To breathe in a world that fears decay.

So light the torches, break the vow,
Kneel to no one not then, not now.
For every myth the holy spun,
Was just the truth they couldn’t outrun.
And if I burn for speaking clear 
Then let me burn  I’ll make it sear.
‘Cause at the end, the gods don’t win 
It’s the cursed who rise,
and rename sin.

The City of Melancholy
                                                                by Parth Joshi

The city of melancholy,
where dreams rot in the cracks of forgotten pavements,
where hope is traded for survival,
where longing cannot name itself 
because it has been silenced too long.
The smiles are masks held by trembling hands.
In the hush beneath the traffic and the laughter,
there is a sound that never leaves, if only you dare to hear it:
the silence of the unseen,
the grieving of those whose lives are 
measured not in moments of joy,
but in how much pain they can endure 
before vanishing completely.

Who is happy here?
Is a pregnant woman at a construction site,
carrying bricks heavier than her future,
her back bending under the weight of survival, happy?
Who is happy here?
Is a little girl running barefoot on pavements to sell balloons,
clutching balloons that 
once should have been hers to hold with joy,
but now belong to strangers—
her childhood sold piece by piece, one balloon at a time,
so her siblings might eat for a single night, happy?
Who is happy here?

A man with ribs cutting sharp against his skin,
walks a tightrope strung between buildings,
the city stretching below him,
a city that does not see him, that does not care—
hurrying forward, indifferent,
as if lives like his are nothing but shadows?
Is a Woman with a vanishing home happy?
She lives in a crumbling house 
in the slum where the roof weeps when it rains.
Her house stood on the bones of her labor—
years of grueling work that wore her down,
treating her less like a person and more like a machine,
in city that moves indifferently like she doesn't exist.

Around her are the glass skyscrapers towering high,
their shiny walls reflecting the very slum she calls home.
Tonight, a heavy wind pulls aside her torn curtains
just enough for her to see one of those towering buildings.
she sees countless lives like hers—
small, unnoticed in the reflection of the glass windows.
The city moves on, indifferent and vast,
a silent witness to the lives it crushes without a second glance.

Tomorrow, her house will be torn down.
She has no papers, no proof that she ever claimed this place.
Her body aches from a lifetime of hard work,
and her hands tremble like they always do after the endless labor.
This is her last night here.

Tomorrow, she could be cast out—
left on the streets or sent back to a village
that stopped feeling like home long ago,
the very place she once begged to escape.
This city—this world—is a cage of illusions;
no matter how far you run,
you're only trapped in a bigger cage that holds the smaller one tight
Ask about the beauty of rain to the nurse in the pale light of the ward,
who watched rain crawl down the cracked walls,
carrying with it the stories of broken homes outside.
The soul haunted by its own aching hollow.
Yet somehow, through every fracture, the sky refuses to turn away.
Rain, for some it is verses whispered on old glass,
poetry on a windowpane,
to others, it's the prayer, a plea for shelter,
a roof that doesn't weep.
The difference between survival and sorrow.
The world's fairness, measured in water, is always uneven.
Isn't it cruel?
How world can be beautiful while you are breaking,
when your world has splintered with hunger and fatigue
and the hush of despair.
Lives unravel in the shadows
Today, I saw a little girl standing at the edge of a moving train's door,
her head barely held up beneath a basket of flowers,
her feet were bruised with walking barefeet on the cruel roads
of melancholic city
I wanted to ask her if those flowers were sewn with threads of sorrow,
dreams, or a fierce love for life,
somehow none of the answer could have made me happy
so I stood there.
But before I could speak, before the train could even stop,
she had already stepped away, vanishing into the station—
leaving behind only the silence of unanswered questions.

Next to the glass-paned window,
In our shared apartment:
A house of dreams, a place to grow.
I kept it on all night,
When you were staying out late,
Tapping on the table.

Warm still, with your comfort meal,
Made with salvaged ingredients and salt
From the refrigerator.
Nursed a glass of single malt,
Post-it notes saying “I’ll be back,”
Magnets rearranged out of order,
Dust motes against the moonlight,
The curtains open just a crack.

Come back, lover.
There’s dinner ready:
Even if in a foreign land,
Hot soup warms your cold, cold hands.
Brittle hearts, little shards,
Fickle starts, settle apart
In the dust motes, in the first notes

All rhetorical.

Pancakes and maple no longer sweet,
When we no longer dance to familiar beats.
Your promises like the kitchen clock:
Tick, tick… but who walks the talk?
